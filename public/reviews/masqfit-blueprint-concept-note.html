The opportunity

MasqFit is an open-source iOS app that uses facial measurements and contributed fit-test results to recommend respirators. Kanro’s original $50,000 grant supported its development. Its current scan-based workflow depends on compatible iPhone hardware. Ordinary RGB cameras could extend access to Android and web users while producing reusable evidence about low-cost facial measurement.

The proposed public-health benefit is a more informed shortlist of respirator models and sizes to test. Recommendations complement formal fit testing. The research will measure selection usefulness rather than infer protection from geometric accuracy alone.

Recent experimental progress

A working experimental pipeline now reconstructs facial surfaces from multiple ordinary-camera photos, applies Gaussian smoothing by 3D convolution, and sets physical scale using a tape-measured bitragion–subnasale arc: from one ear’s tragion, beneath the nose, to the other tragion.

Existing iOS recommender: top-1 and top-3 results

The recommender used with MasqFit’s iOS workflow reports a 51.7% top-1 hit rate and an 82.2% top-3 hit rate in retrospective evaluation. These are unweighted averages across five user-grouped cross-validation folds, among held-out users with at least one positive fit label.

Example: if a person’s previously tested observations rank as negative, negative, positive, then top-1 misses and top-3 succeeds. Top-3 means at least one positive result appears within the first three ranked observations; it does not require all three to fit.

Live dashboard checked November 9, 2026 · Model November 8, 2026. The candidate set consists of recorded test observations, with mixed-source labels and possible repeats, rather than three new masks from the full catalog. Prospective success for new users remains to be measured.

Improve selection with features tailored to respirator style

Alongside broader access, test whether different facial features improve recommendations for filtering facepiece respirators (FFRs) and quarter-mask elastomerics. The existing iOS recommender already uses mask family/style terms, while its facial geometry is summarized by a perimeter proxy. The new work would retain local geometry separately: for example, cheek/chin dimensions for flexible lower panels, nasal slope and bridge shape for the upper seal, and dimensions matched to a quarter-mask’s actual contact footprint.

These are hypotheses to test on feature-linked human fit outcomes. Compare style-dependent features with the current model using the same held-out people and candidate observations; preserve the baseline where added features do not help. Quarter-mask elastomeric work begins as a bounded feasibility analysis, with a larger trial dependent on adequate data and separately agreed scope.

Which approach delivers the most useful result for its cost?

The research will compare photogrammetry with 3D smoothing, direct-photo-to-FLAME fitting, and the existing iPhone-only ARKit workflow on the same people. The working hypothesis is a tradeoff between local-shape accuracy and speed, capture effort, and processing cost—not a demonstrated ordering of the three methods.

For healthcare personnel onboarding or respirator-selection campaigns, measure time to a usable recommendation, device/compute and staff costs, retries, and quantitative fit-test outcomes. Ask how much more photogrammetry costs than direct fitting, and whether any extra accuracy changes the shortlist or produces a material increase in first-choice fit-test passes. A small geometric improvement with unchanged decisions may offer little operational value.

No comparable per-user cost or head-to-head accuracy gain has yet been established. The study will report cost per usable recommendation and per verified first-choice fit-test pass, with uncertainty. A numerical example and the comparison protocol appear in the detailed proposal.

What funding would establish

  1. Feasibility and calibration: freeze landmark definitions and capture settings, test smoothing and scale sensitivity, and complete a 20-adult geometry pilot.
  2. Independent measurement validation: expand to 120 adults total, with 60 development participants including the pilot and 60 untouched test participants. Compare repeated captures with independently acquired physical/reference measurements, across devices and face shapes.
  3. Selection usefulness: contingent on measurement performance, enroll 60 separate adults in a prospective quantitative fit-testing pilot, comparing the first frozen camera-based recommendation with each participant’s initial choice. Release an Android/web research beta within validated conditions.

Deliverables include reproducible measurement code, a documented benchmark and failure analysis, device/usability results, a prospective fit-pilot report, and public methods and non-identifying aggregate findings. Participant images and identifiable meshes require separate consent for sharing.
