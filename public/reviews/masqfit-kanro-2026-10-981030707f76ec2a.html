Proposal to Kanro · Review draft · Updated October 22, 2026

Make better respirator selection accessible on Android and the web

Develop and validate ordinary-camera facial measurements for MasqFit, then test whether its recommendations help people find respirators that fit.

Edderic Ugaddan · Breathesafe LLC · Follow-on to Kanro’s $50,000 award in 2024

Shared for feedback before submission to Kanro. Please send review comments to Edderic.

Draft for discussion Proposed duration: approximately 14 calendar months, including parental leave

Proposed request: $196,800, including $16,800 for eight weeks of paid parental leave.

The full project combines a measured library of printed faces, synthetic training data, independent human measurement validation, a prospective fit-testing pilot, and Android/web delivery. Further development depends on passing predefined measurement and usability gates.

The research budget remains $180,000, including a $50,000 feasibility phase. An initial award of $66,800 would cover that phase and the separately itemized parental-leave allowance.

Planning assumptions, sample sizes, compensation, and success thresholds below are provisional. This is a new research proposal; the budget is not a vendor quotation or a guarantee of technical success.

1. Objective and remaining access barrier

The original 2024 proposal set out to make it easier for people to find respirators likely to fit their faces. Kanro’s $50,000 support helped advance that work into MasqFit and the Breathesafe recommender. The next step is to remove the need for a compatible iPhone while establishing stronger evidence about measurement quality and recommendation usefulness.

MasqFit currently captures facial measurements through Apple’s TrueDepth/ARKit workflow. People without that hardware cannot use the same scan-based experience. I propose researching whether a standard RGB camera, available on Android phones and through web browsers, can recover the facial geometry needed by the recommender. A short guided capture, calibrated using one tape-measured facial arc where needed, would replace specialized depth hardware. This adds a modest manual step using a widely available, inexpensive tool in exchange for a directly measured scale reference.

Fit remains individual: leakage around a respirator’s seal can compromise protection, and a model/size that fits one person may not fit another. Formal fit testing evaluates the worn respirator, including its seal. MasqFit’s purpose is to make the search for suitable options more informed and accessible; its recommendations complement fit testing. [1]

The project has three linked research questions:

  1. Measurement: Can ordinary-camera captures estimate an ARKit-compatible subset of facial landmarks and distances in millimeters accurately enough for respirator ranking?
  2. Prediction: Does representing nose geometry independently of total face perimeter improve fit prediction, and does that value vary between filtering facepiece and elastomeric respirators?
  3. Usefulness: Do camera-derived recommendations preserve useful ranking behavior and improve fit-test outcomes relative to a participant’s own initial respirator choice?

At a glance: build, measure, and test

The generator creates virtual faces. Selected faces can be printed. The camera estimator learns to recover facial geometry from images. We then test the measurements and recommendations on people.

2. Current project status and results

MasqFit is available on iOS, and Breathesafe provides a web interface for respirator information and recommendations. The project combines facial measurements with contributed fit-related outcomes, and its code is available under GPLv3. Its catalog includes child-sized options and several respirator standards, with breathability information for part of the catalog. These features establish the existing foundation; accuracy for children or individual demographic groups has not yet been demonstrated.

I have been invited by CDC/NIOSH’s National Personal Protective Technology Laboratory (NPPTL) to present the research and engineering behind MasqFit at the November 2026 meeting of the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine’s Standing Committee on Personal Protective Equipment for Workplace Safety and Health, during a session exploring facial imaging technologies and their applications to respirator fit.

What camera measurements must support

The current inspected scoring path uses four facial path sums—nose, upper cheek, mid-cheek, and chin—compressed into a perimeter proxy and compared with mask perimeter, alongside fit-family, style, and strap-type effects. It does not independently use every collected facial feature. The four paths currently comprise 46 short segments connecting 46 distinct ARKit vertices. The proposed mapping will preserve those definitions for comparison while adding a separately evaluated nose-feature predictor. [3]

A limitation worth addressing: two faces with the same perimeter proxy receive the same geometry-dependent score for a given mask even if their nose shapes differ. Although the existing nose_mm path contributes to the sum, it is not independently represented as nose-bridge height or lateral nose slope. The new work will test whether retaining local nasal geometry improves prediction beyond the perimeter baseline.

The research combines established methods rather than assuming that a new model trained on many faces will automatically become accurate. The studies below support specific design choices, with important limits on transfer to MasqFit.

4. Proposed research and development

The two diagrams above summarize the development and human-validation workflows. The sections below define how each stage will be measured and delivered.

A. Define landmarks and establish physical reference measurements

I already own a 3D printer. I will use it to create approximately 20–30 facial models spanning selected widths, lengths, nose-bridge shapes, and cheek/chin geometries. A small physical library is sufficient for controlled experiments; most training diversity will come from digital rendering.

Each model will have a versioned correspondence between selected ARKit surface locations and the reference mesh. An ARKit vertex is a topology-specific surface point, not necessarily an anatomical landmark that someone can reliably identify with a pen. Stable anatomical anchors will support mesh registration, with documented surface/barycentric positions for intervening path vertices. We will validate all 46 points required by the existing four paths, or explicitly document a reduced representation and separately validate any learned conversion. Matching labels alone will not be considered interoperability.

The reference process will compare three things: nominal CAD geometry, independently measured printed geometry, and camera estimates. Calibrated calipers will measure accessible straight-line spans; flexible tape will measure specified surface paths. These are different quantities. Existing ARKit aggregates are sums of short 3D chords, so their reference values will be computed from corresponding points on an independently scaled mesh and cross-checked against compatible physical measurements.

Printer scaling, warping, landmark placement, caliper pressure, and tape positioning will be recorded. Two raters will independently repeat a subset of measurements. A small metrology/reference-scan allowance will support independent checks beyond the geometry used to generate the print.

Avoiding visible-marker shortcuts: physical marks will establish reference locations, but inference captures will also be made without visible marks, or markers will be masked from training images. Final human evaluation will use unmarked captures. The deployed estimator must work without users drawing the target landmarks on their faces.

Deliverables: landmark atlas and mapping schema, including additional nasal reference points where required; measurement protocol; measured print library; reference uncertainty report; and a scale-recovery experiment on faces with similar shape but different physical size.

B. Build a constrained synthetic-face generator and camera estimator

The generator will begin with consented or appropriately licensed face meshes and produce anatomically plausible variations. Planned controls include nasal root breadth, bridge/protrusion, facial width and depth, jaw width, chin shape, asymmetry, and overall size. Correlated shape variation will be preferred to unrestricted independent stretching. Extreme or implausible changes will be flagged rather than treated as representative people.

Landmark annotations will deform with each mesh so that positions, physical scale, pairwise distances, and path sums remain available as labels. An initial target is 5,000–20,000 distinct digital shapes and 100,000–500,000 rendered frames, expanded only if learning curves show benefit. Render counts are an engineering target, not the effective number of independent human subjects.

Rendering will vary pose, illumination, skin appearance, camera intrinsics and distortion, backgrounds, expression, compression, blur, and occlusion. Selected prints will provide repeatable real-camera captures across devices. Printed material has a different visual appearance from skin, so this data will supplement photorealistic rendering and human captures rather than replace them.

The learning target will be a consistent set of head-relative 3D landmarks plus explicit scale, or an equivalent representation from which the same physical distances can be calculated. Training will compare coordinate loss, millimeter distance loss for selected pairs, path-sum error, and multiview consistency. Shape-normalized error will be reported separately from metric-scale error.

Predicted distance: d̂(i,j) = ‖p̂ᵢ − p̂ⱼ‖₂, in millimeters
Physical-distance loss: Σ w(i,j) · |d̂(i,j) − d_reference(i,j)|
Path estimate: sum of the same specified short chords used by MasqFit

We will compare an existing landmark/mesh baseline, a synthetic-trained model, and a synthetic-plus-real adaptation model. Ablations will test the incremental value of printed captures and targeted shape perturbations. Whole source identities and all derived shapes/renders will remain within one data partition; synthetic relatives of a held-out person will never enter training.

Absolute scale is an explicit research question. A single uncalibrated image cannot uniquely distinguish a small face nearby from a larger face farther away. A short multiview capture can improve shape recovery but does not by itself establish absolute size. We will prioritize a tape-calibrated capture, compare it with a learned-scale baseline, and retain an in-scene known-size reference as another option. An in-scene reference must be correctly localized in 3D; holding it arbitrarily nearer the camera is insufficient. Generic iris size, average face size, or a canonical mesh will not be treated as a measured scale anchor. [12]

B1. Use one tape measurement to anchor absolute scale

A practical candidate is the bitragion–subnasale arc: the facial surface path from one tragion, near the front of the ear, across the subnasale beneath the nose, to the opposite tragion. This is an established tape-measured anthropometric dimension. The proposed guided workflow will explain the endpoints and path, record millimeters, and ask for repeated readings with light, consistent tape contact. The measurement is intended to anchor scale, not to replace the camera’s estimate of detailed facial shape. [21]

Scale s = tape-measured arc length (mm) / corresponding reconstructed arc length (model units)
Metric landmark pᵢ(mm) = s × pᵢ(model units)
Metric pair distance d(i,j) = s × ‖pᵢ − pⱼ‖₂

This conversion assumes a reconstructed 3D shape with a consistent geometry, correct up to one global scale factor. Raw 2D pixel distances, or normalized x/y/z outputs with incompatible axis scales, cannot simply be treated as such a reconstruction. The model arc must match the tape’s endpoints and surface route rather than a straight chord or an arbitrary shortest mesh path. Ear-adjacent tragion points may lie outside a standard frontal face mesh, so feasibility includes guided side views, additional landmarks or surface coverage, and comparison with an easier-to-capture alternative anchor if required.

One reliable arc can resolve the remaining global scale ambiguity; it cannot correct distorted depth, incorrect local nasal shape, or a mismatched arc definition. For example, a 5 mm tape error on a 300 mm arc introduces about 1.7% scale error if reconstructed shape is otherwise correct. The same relative error propagates to all inferred lengths, while reconstructed arc error contributes separately. Uniform rescaling leaves angles unchanged, so lateral nose slope still requires its own shape-validation benchmark.

Calibration experiment: compare self-measured and assisted tape readings, repeated sessions, and tape-calibrated versus learned-scale and in-scene-reference capture. Use printed models to test known-size recovery, then assess real users’ completion time, need for help, repeatability, capture failures, and accuracy of distances not used as the anchor. Compare against independently scaled reference scans and independent measurements; matching the input arc after rescaling is a construction property, not validation evidence. Reference meshes will not be scaled using the same entered arc being evaluated.

Deliverable: a documented tape-calibration procedure and a decision about whether its additional user effort produces a worthwhile reduction in millimeter error and recommendation instability. This experiment is included within the existing A/B/C tools, scale-recovery, and human-validation allocations; the funding request is unchanged.

The project remains camera-only in the sense of requiring no depth sensor. A validated camera-plus-tape workflow is a primary route to delivery; fully reference-free millimeter inference is an optional experimental improvement rather than a prerequisite for Android/web access.

Deliverables: generator and labeled render pipeline; baseline comparisons; training/evaluation scripts; model and data documentation; and a feasibility decision about the simplest viable scale/capture method.

B2. Compare local geometry and a compact feature set with face perimeter

This task adds a concrete improvement to the current recommender alongside the camera-access work. Amanda Abbott’s elastomeric guidance motivates bridge height; Yu and Griffin’s FFR study motivates lateral nose slope. These are distinct constructs, not alternative names for the same distance. Neither is automatically equivalent to the existing ARKit nose-path sum. [18] [19]

Yu et al.’s five-measurement model provides a concrete compact candidate: mid-nose slope, nose protrusion, lateral zygomatic–gonion length, gonion–menton length, and ear height–top. We will test this feature set as an alternative to perimeter, as well as nasal additions to the existing model. The published study used one respirator design; its coefficients and feature ranking will not be assumed to transfer to other masks. [23]

Flo Mask Pro provides a practical product example: its adult mask has Med/High and Low/Med Nose Bridge variants, supported by a printable bridge-sizing guide. This motivates recording nose-bridge variant identity in the catalog and testing whether measured nasal features help distinguish suitable variants, including for faces with similar total perimeter. Variant-specific fit records should remain distinguishable when related products share a fit family. The product options support the design rationale; they do not establish a universal bridge-height threshold or the benefit of the proposed predictor. [20]

During feasibility, define a small set of repeatable features: the existing nose-path length as an independent predictor, physical bridge height/protrusion relative to specified local reference planes, and left/right lateral nose slope. Record distance features in millimeters and slopes as explicitly defined angles or dimensionless ratios. Abbott’s subjective low/medium/high category will remain separate from an objectively measured distance; any mapping between them requires data. Reproducing the published slope measure requires the paper’s full geometric definition; a locally defined alternative will be labeled as such.

The landmark atlas will include the extra nasal points or surface patches needed to estimate those features, with paired physical/scan references and a documented ARKit mapping where feasible. A few existing perimeter vertices may not capture the relevant local slope. Compare repeatability and error for nasal distances and angles separately; accurate total perimeter alone will not establish accurate nose geometry. Synthetic examples will deliberately vary nasal shape while keeping total perimeter approximately constant, testing whether the measurement pipeline can recover the distinction.

Incremental prediction experiment: compare the existing perimeter model with (i) perimeter plus the independent nose-path value, (ii) perimeter plus bridge height, and (iii) perimeter plus lateral nose slope. A combined model is secondary and contingent on sufficient data. Preserve the baseline mask-family/style/strap terms, use regularization, and compare on the same users and tested options. Feature selection, imputation, and tuning stay inside participant-grouped training folds, with nested tuning or a separate development split. Report paired changes in top-1/top-3/top-5 relevance, discrimination, and calibration with participant-level uncertainty, retaining the historical conditional metric’s eligibility definition for retrospective comparisons.

A smaller measurement target for the camera pipeline

Five useful measurements could reduce annotation and automation effort compared with reproducing every existing path vertex. The estimator can target the required landmarks and local surface geometry using a CNN/heatmap detector or another suitable image model, followed by geometric measurement extraction. We will compare this targeted estimator with the broader reconstruction approach. Fewer outputs are a testable simplification, not proof that the points are easier to locate: jaw and ear-adjacent landmarks may need side views, and a slope or surface path may require more than two visible points.

Before labeling, obtain the paper’s supplementary definitions and specify endpoints, reference planes, surface versus straight-line distances, and units for each feature. In particular, a feature named “slope” will not automatically be converted into an angle. Validate repeat annotation, reference measurement, visibility, and camera error for each construct. Treat the published exponentiated coefficients as study-specific associations, not universal weights or directly comparable feature-importance scores. [23]

This design distinguishes a better feature representation from a better measurement method. It also permits useful findings if compact reference measurements predict fit well but automated capture still needs improvement.

Fit new coefficients on consented, feature-linked human outcomes rather than reuse the single-design paper’s regression equation. A multi-mask recommender must condition on mask identity or design and allow supported feature-by-mask effects; a face-only score cannot rank different masks for the same person. Keep the training population, candidate options, participant splits, outcome definitions, and tuning allowance comparable. A perimeter-plus-compact hybrid is exploratory and will not be added simply because it has more predictors.

Compare predictive relevance, calibration, measurement error, annotation time, capture time, and failure rates. Start with reference extraction and a small targeted-estimator feasibility experiment within the existing B2 allocation. Full automation is conditional on useful prediction and acceptable capture complexity; any work beyond that bounded experiment will be re-estimated before expanding scope.

Analyze filtering facepieces and elastomerics separately and consider feature-by-mask-family effects only where observations support them. Existing records may not contain the required geometry; new feature-linked fit data must come from consenting people, and derived synthetic faces will never receive invented fit outcomes. The base prospective pilot will prioritize filtering facepieces within its equipment/protocol allowance. Elastomeric analysis will use suitable existing or newly consented records when sufficient; otherwise report insufficient evidence and specify a separately scoped follow-up.

Before the prospective pilot, select and freeze one recommender version using development evidence alone. If local nasal features do not show reliable incremental value, retain the perimeter baseline. Do not expand the seven-option prospective test union to test every model variant; evaluate alternatives retrospectively on the same observed outcomes with their selection limits stated. The independently validated camera estimator and the recommender’s feature choices remain separate research decisions.

Deliverables and allocation: feature definitions and measurement protocol, reproducible compact-feature and nasal comparisons against perimeter alone, targeted-landmark automation feasibility, respirator-class-specific findings, and a documented model-selection decision. Allocate 10 of component B’s 40 PI days ($6,000 of its $32,000 total) to this bounded work; the remaining B allocation is $26,000. This is an internal subdivision, not an additional charge. Local-feature reference collection and error analysis are included in A and C; the budget does not add a separate elastomeric trial.

C. Validate measurement accuracy on real human faces

Recruit approximately 120 adults for geometry validation: 20 pilot participants, 40 additional development participants, and 60 untouched test participants. The pilot is part of the 60-person development set, not an additional sample. Target variation in face shape, skin tone, age, gender, facial hair, and device access, while acknowledging that this size cannot establish precise accuracy for every subgroup.

Collect unmarked RGB images/video, repeated capture sessions, paired ARKit measurements where available, and independently scaled photogrammetry or another reference scan. Selected caliper/tape measurements will check reference geometry. Repeat the reference measurements for a subset with a second rater. Where photogrammetry is used as a reference, collect it independently of the input capture to avoid validating a reconstruction against itself.

Use approximately six Android devices spanning lower, middle, and higher camera capability, plus desktop/laptop webcams and a supported iPhone reference. Reserve at least one device model from model development. Report performance by device and image quality, with all captures of a person assigned to the same partition.

Following Nissen et al.’s controlled measurement approach, include repeated captures across phone-to-face distances, viewing angles, and session duration/device thermal state. Use reproducible positioning aids for printed-face experiments. Evaluate the actual facial spans and paths separately from camera-to-face distance. [22]

Before examining the 60-person test set, lock the estimator, preprocessing, landmark map, quality thresholds, and analysis. Evaluate millimeter bias, mean absolute error, 95th-percentile absolute error, repeatability, Bland–Altman agreement, and capture completion/abstention. Rigid alignment may remove pose for point comparisons; any scale alignment used for a shape-only diagnostic will be explicitly excluded from absolute millimeter accuracy claims.

For compatibility, compare camera and ARKit paths on the same people, while keeping the independent physical reference as the accuracy anchor. ARKit is the incumbent interface, not error-free ground truth. Validate the additional nasal distances and slope estimates independently, then propagate their measurement errors through both the perimeter baseline and the nose-augmented candidate. Angular tolerances will be set during the pilot from reference repeatability and ranking sensitivity, before the untouched geometry test set is opened.

Deliverables: locked human measurement benchmark, device/subgroup diagnostics, comparison against ARKit, and a decision to proceed, narrow supported conditions, or stop deployment work.

D. Connect geometry to human fit outcomes

Recruit approximately 60 additional adults who have not previously used MasqFit for a prospective pilot. These participants are separate from the 120-person geometry cohort and will not be used to tune either the camera estimator or the recommendation model before their outcomes are analyzed.

Each participant will choose an initial respirator before seeing recommendations, from a predefined locally available set. A frozen recommender will then generate top-three options using camera-derived measurements and, where paired reference capture is available, top-three options using ARKit. Test the union of the initial choice and the two recommendation lists—up to seven distinct model/size combinations per participant. Duplicates are tested once and represented consistently in the analysis.

Randomize test order to reduce learning and fatigue effects. Standardize donning assistance and record adjustments. The fit-test operator will be blinded to the source of each recommendation where practicable. Use one predefined quantitative protocol and appropriate equipment configuration for the respirator class, recording exercises, instrument setup, and continuous fit factors as well as protocol-defined pass/fail. Formal qualitative testing may provide supplementary results if needed, but its binary outcomes will be reported separately; it will not be silently pooled with quantitative fit factors. [1]

Primary pilot comparison: paired quantitative pass rates for the first camera-based recommendation versus the participant’s initial choice. Secondary comparisons: at least one passing respirator among the camera top-three; camera versus ARKit recommendation outcomes; masks tried and time to a passing option; completion and abstention. Comparing three recommendations with one initial choice offers unequal numbers of attempts, so that result will be labeled as a practical search strategy rather than a controlled top-one comparison.

Report all enrolled participants in the study flow, including technical failures, people without any passing mask in the tested set, withdrawals, and missing outcomes. A per-protocol analysis can accompany an all-enrolled operational analysis with predefined handling of abstentions. Use paired effect estimates and confidence intervals, not the historical conditional top-k denominator.

This is a pilot sized for feasibility and effect estimation. At n=60, a single proportion near 50% has an approximate 95% margin of error of 13 percentage points; precision for paired differences depends on discordant outcomes. It is not a powered non-inferiority trial, a study of infection reduction, or a basis for claiming universal subgroup effectiveness. A larger study would require a separate sample-size calculation and budget.

Why fit labels come from people: synthetic faces provide known geometry but no observed respirator outcome. A shape-perturbed derivative of a real person will not inherit that person’s fit labels. PLA prints are rigid, and TPU flexibility alone does not reproduce tissue mechanics, friction, sealing, or facial movement. Any exploratory mannequin fit measurements would remain a separate noisy engineering dataset, outside the proposed human fit endpoints. [15] [16]

Vollmer et al.’s advanced-headform study supports combining geometry and fit information, but calls for human validation. Its 98% headform prediction-set coverage is a different endpoint from MasqFit’s conditional top-k relevance and the proposed prospective fit-test outcomes. We will report those endpoints separately and test sensitivity to realistic measurement error. [24]

Deliverables: preregistered pilot protocol, a consented geometry–fit linkage, participant-flow report, effect estimates with uncertainty, and recommendations for a larger trial.

E. Deliver validated Android and web access

Begin with an Android-capable browser prototype to validate capture usability without maintaining two independent vision implementations. The full scope includes a web capture experience and an Android application or installable client sharing the estimator and measurement contract. The precise packaging will be chosen after benchmarking camera controls, runtime support, and offline/on-device feasibility.

Return versioned measurements in millimeters, quality flags, supported-device information, and provenance identifying camera versus ARKit capture. Preserve the existing four path definitions rather than relabeling unrelated MediaPipe points with ARKit IDs. Maintain paired compatibility tests for shared distances, aggregate sums, coordinate conventions, and model versions.

Deploy capture-quality checks for pose, blur, occlusion, scale-reference placement, and unsupported conditions. If validation fails, request recapture or offer a documented alternative measurement route. Any alternative must itself be validated before it is represented as interchangeable. 6. Privacy and research participation

Routine app use and research data collection will remain distinct. The proposed research may require retaining consented photographs, videos, and 3D meshes for labeling and validation—more sensitive information than derived measurements. This is a change in research needs, not a claim that existing baseline consent already covers it. Separate study consent and an appropriate ethics/IRB determination will be established before recruitment or retention.

The study will initially recruit adults. Pediatric validation is a future extension requiring its own consent, safeguarding, sampling, and budget. Research participation will be optional, with pseudonymous identifiers, encrypted storage, access limits, a defined retention/deletion schedule, and clear withdrawal limits once a model or non-identifying aggregate has been released.

Where feasible, deployed inference will run on the device and transmit only derived measurements. Server processing, if necessary, will require an explicit data-flow decision and disclosure. Full face images and meshes will not be called anonymous simply because names are removed; face-derived synthetic data may also retain identity information. Public releases will prioritize code, protocols, and aggregate results, with data sharing bounded by consent and reidentification risk.

Appendix: evaluation and reproducibility details

  • Version everything: landmark map, ARKit topology assumptions, units, axes/handedness, capture software, device/camera, reference calibration, generator seed identities, model weights, and recommender catalog/version.
  • Separate targets: 2D landmark localization, relative 3D shape, absolute millimeter scale, aggregate path accuracy, ranking stability, and human fit outcomes each receive their own analysis. Success at one does not imply success at the next.
  • Preserve local shape: distinguish the ARKit nose-path sum, objective bridge-height/protrusion distances, subjective bridge category, and lateral slope. Compare nasal predictors against perimeter alone with participant-separated model selection; report FFR and elastomeric evidence separately.
  • Protect independent evaluation: split by real person and originating synthetic identity before fitting normalizers, imputers, correspondence adjustments, or calibration. The geometry test set and prospective fit cohort remain outside development. Repeated images are not counted as independent participants.
  • Compare fair baselines: existing landmark/mesh output with a documented scale method, the proposed estimator with and without synthetic augmentation, and paired ARKit measurements. Compare recommender versions on the same evaluation population; the historical conditional hit rate is context, not a target to optimize by changing eligibility.
  • Report failures: counts screened, consented, captured, excluded, and evaluated; reasons for exclusion; unsupported devices; missing references; and abstention rates. Use participant-level resampling for repeated captures and paired intervals for fit comparisons.
  • Quantify reference uncertainty: repeatability, between-rater disagreement, printing error, reference-scan scaling, and soft-tissue movement. A tape surface arc and a caliper chord will never be treated as the same label.
  • Test calibration independently: document the exact tape path, repeated readings, reconstructed arc, scale factor, and whether assistance was needed. Evaluate other physical distances against an independently scaled reference, rather than counting agreement with the calibration arc itself as accuracy evidence.
  • Keep synthetic labels honest: use CAD-derived metric positions and observed physical print measurements as distinct sources; never create synthetic fit-test “truth” by propagating a parent face’s outcome to altered geometry.
  • Prespecify human endpoints: register primary comparison and missing-data handling before fit outcomes are collected, with no subgroup efficacy claim from a handful of participants. Publish a larger-study power analysis informed by this pilot’s discordance and attrition.
