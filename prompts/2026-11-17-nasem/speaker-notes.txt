9. Keep the nose path separate from total perimeter. · 1:05
Previously, nose, upper cheek, mid-cheek and chin paths were summed into a single perimeter proxy. Two people with the same total could receive the same score despite different nose shapes. The perimeter-model logit is a times the squared signed perimeter difference, plus b times the difference, plus c and a strap-type offset. The signed difference is face perimeter minus mask perimeter, divided by ten to convert millimeters to centimeters. Face perimeter sums the nose, upper-cheek, mid-cheek and chin paths. Coefficients a, b and c combine learned fit-family and style terms; a is constrained negative. A logit is the model score before the sigmoid transformation; the displayed fit score is sigmoid of the new logit, not an independently calibrated probability. The new model retains the total-perimeter term and adds a separate squared nose mismatch. The face nose path is an input; the mask and style preferred nose distance is inferred from fit labels, not measured mask geometry. Across three initializations on the same five user-grouped folds, precision rose from 56.4% to 63.9%, recall rose from 58.0% to 59.4%, and log loss fell from 1.108 to 0.931. Precision asks: among tests called fitting, how many had positive labels? Recall asks: among positive labels, how many were found? NPV asks: among observations predicted not to fit, how many have negative labels? It rises from 85.7% to 86.6% at the fixed 50 percent cutoff. It is true negatives divided by true negatives plus false negatives; no predicted negatives makes it undefined. Log loss penalizes confident mistakes using the original continuous scores, not their binarized versions. Lower log loss means better overall probabilistic scoring but can reflect changes in discrimination or calibration; it does not establish calibration improvement alone. Higher precision at the fixed cutoff means a smaller false-positive fraction among predicted fits. It does not prove that scores fell for every nonfit, rose for every fit, or improved the absolute number of false positives or true positives; a more selective predictor can also improve precision. A recall interval including zero leaves the direction uncertain, rather than establishing equal recall. All classification metrics use the fixed 50 percent cutoff except log loss, which uses the scores directly. These are averages of pooled held-out metrics from each initialization, not training scores or independent replicated datasets. Brackets show pointwise 95 percent percentile intervals from 10,000 paired participant-cluster bootstrap samples: sample 49 people with replacement and retain all their tests, using identical draws for models and initializations. Compute each metric per initialization then average; do not average probabilities. These are conditional on saved fits and folds, not full retraining intervals. Precision and log-loss paired difference intervals exclude zero; recall, NPV and F1 intervals include zero. The mixed cohort contains measured and imputed ARKit paths. The regression used for imputation was fitted before CV, so full-pipeline generalization is not independently established. Measured-only training on 615 tests from 18 people did not reproduce the gain: average F1 fell from .620 to .578. The new log loss remains worse than the prevalence-only baseline of approximately .588; calibration needs separate reliability assessment. Labels mix evidence sources and repeated user-mask tests can remain. This is exploratory internal evidence. Shared nose sensitivity is now the training default in code; style-specific sensitivity remains experimental.

15. The tradeoff: metric scale, speed, and reach. · 0:45
These are three different starting points. In our TrueDepth-based ARKit workflow, the mesh has metric coordinates, so I can calculate a distance in meters and convert to millimeters. MediaPipe is very useful for real-time landmark detection across camera platforms, but its standard output is normalized image coordinates and relative depth. Converting normalized coordinates to pixels does not tell me the physical size of the face. Google also offers a Face Geometry representation with canonical metric units; that is not an independent measurement of the particular person’s absolute size. Photogrammetry reconstructs a surface from multiple images. It supports ordinary cameras, but still needs physical scale and anatomical landmarks. The roughly 45 seconds here is an earlier report for raw reconstruction, not a benchmark of the updated capture, reconstruction, smoothing and landmarking workflow.

Three components were selected before outcome evaluation, not by choosing the best held-out score. This is a project-specific PCA, not the NIOSH PCA panel. 23. Where do a, b and c come from? · Backup
The displayed coefficients combine a fit-family component and a mask-style component. The internal names alpha_mask_raw and beta_gamma_mask refer to fit-family categories in the current training pipeline. For the current constrained parameterization, the two raw alpha parameters are exponentiated and negated, then added, so the effective quadratic coefficient a is negative. The linear and intercept coefficients each add family and style values. Training learns all these underlying parameters jointly by minimizing binary cross-entropy on observed fit outcomes. The strap category contributes a separate additive offset. The with-nose model also subtracts a separately learned nose-mismatch penalty. The peak minus b over two a refers to the perimeter term with categories fixed; it is not a claim about the best physical mask geometry or the optimum of the full model when the nose term changes. The coefficients are learned associations, not physical constants. Legacy parameter files can bypass the newer negative-curvature transformation; the equation describes the current training parameterization.

24. Both traditional models include squared terms. · Backup
Both traditional comparison models use linear and squared geometry terms. For the direct model, the six dimensions are standardized before squaring. For PCA, the same standardized dimensions are projected into three components, and those component scores are squared. This is not PCA applied to squared raw dimensions. The shared design includes one-hot fit-family, style and strap categories, standardized mask perimeter p and its square, each geometry basis term multiplied by p, and each geometry basis term multiplied by every category indicator. Thus the squared geometry terms also interact with perimeter and categories; this is not simply a full degree-two polynomial expansion. There are no explicit cross-products between different facial dimensions or different PCs. Squared PCs can nevertheless encode combinations of the original dimensions, because each PC is a linear combination. Both models are L2 logistic regression with C=1; coefficient signs are not constrained. This differs from the production ARKit architecture, which explicitly uses a concave quadratic in signed perimeter difference and a separate negative squared nose mismatch. Standardization and PCA are fitted using training people only.

26. From N99 counts to an estimated N95 fit factor. · Backup
Worked example: use final sealed reference 100, giving filtration efficiency 99 percent. An N99 exercise factor of 50 gives leakage 1 minus 0.98 divided by 0.99, or 0.010101. Its reciprocal is 99. For one retained exercise, the harmonic mean is 99, and the project multiplier gives 198. This is illustrative, not an observed participant result or direct N95 measurement. This is the equation used for the project’s N99-to-N95 estimate. F_i is the outside-to-inside particle ratio for an N99-mode exercise. E is the filtration fraction, not a percent; L_i is the inferred fraction of incoming flow that bypasses the filter. The simplified mixture gives one over F_i equals L_i plus one minus L_i times one minus E. Solving gives L_i equals one minus one minus one over F_i divided by E; its reciprocal is the exercise-level leakage estimate. Credit Aaron Collins, MaskNerd, for providing filtration-efficiency data at normal breathing rates through his Master Mask Testing Data Set spreadsheet. The repository selects imported entries identified as Aaron’s data; contributed Normal breathing SEALED measurements are a second source. For each imported E_j, compute an equivalent sealed ratio S_j equals one over one minus E_j. Contributed sealed ratios enter directly. Pool by mask using SQL UNION, which removes identical selected rows, then average the ratios, not efficiencies. For retained exercise maximum M, use mean sealed ratio if it exceeds M; otherwise replace with M plus one. This explicit conditional matters: it is not simply max(mean sealed ratio, M plus one). Convert the final ratio back to E. Apply the inverse leakage formula per exercise and aggregate harmonically, excluding Talking and Normal breathing SEALED. The code multiplies the result by two and uses 100 as its positive-label threshold. The two multiplier is a project bias adjustment intended to improve sensitivity, not part of the physical derivation or a correction applied to direct N95-mode tests. The formula is unstable when the observed ratio approaches the sealed reference, and filtration measured under other conditions may not transfer. Normal-breathing observations are not certification tests at a standardized test flow or proof of another wearer’s fit. This credits the data contribution, not endorsement of our conversion, multiplier or recommender. No spreadsheet measurements are reproduced on this slide; the source is linked. Implementation: N99ModeToN95ModeConverterService.avg_sealed_ffs_sql, n95_mode_sql and call. The historical scatter elsewhere uses saved notebook outputs and does not validate this exact production pooling pipeline.

27. Which questionnaire answers become a failure? · Backup
This slide describes UserSealCheckService, the backend rule used to create recommender labels, rather than a formal fit-test protocol. The sizing question asks: What do you think about the sizing of this mask relative to your face? Its choices are Too big, Somewhere in-between too small and too big, and Too small. The second question asks, while performing a positive-pressure user seal check, how much air movement on your face along the seal of the mask did you feel? Its choices are A lot of air movement, Some air movement, and No air movement. Evaluation order matters. First, Too big or Too small returns fail immediately, even if the air answer is absent or contradicts it. Next, a blank or missing sizing or air answer returns incomplete. This means A lot of air movement without a sizing answer is incomplete, not fail. With both answers present, A lot of air movement returns fail, including when the nonblank sizing value is unrecognized. Middle sizing plus Some air movement or No air movement returns questionnaire pass. Other nonblank combinations return incomplete. Missing, empty or invalid JSON data is also incomplete. qlft_pass_value maps fail to false and both pass and incomplete to NULL. The label is therefore asymmetric: a failed self-report can contribute a proxy negative, but passing questions cannot contribute a positive fit label. Another source on the same test record may still supply a label; consolidation favors negative over positive over unknown. The interface also has negative-pressure questions, but those answers are not read by this backend label service. Some air movement being accepted here is a permissive project classification rule, not guidance that a leaking respirator passes a formal fit test. User seal checks are not substitutes for qualitative or quantitative fit tests. No questionnaire, service or training-label behavior is changed by this slide.

29. From photo landmarks to surface-attached curves. · Backup
The two columns describe related but distinct procedures in the current prototype. RealDenseFace supplies dense per-photo correspondences. Its template vertex identities identify candidate regions, but metric-like 3D coordinates come from registered multi-photo triangulation and the reconstructed surface, not from accepting a fitted FLAME nose shape. Observation gates reject points outside the image, prediction crop or skin mask. Bounded pair hypotheses reject reprojection outliers; raw-surface proximity and at least three visible views are required before attachment. For nose-wing and cheekbone widths, fixed template regions propose supported, height-matched lateral endpoints; visual anatomical review remains necessary. A barycentric attachment stores the mesh triangle and three weights summing to one, so a point can lie inside a triangle rather than snapping to a vertex.
