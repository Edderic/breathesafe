7. What does “fit” mean in this dataset? · 0:30
Participant access to fit-testing equipment varies. Some have access only to standard/N99-mode particle counting or qualitative testing. Ideally this project would have direct N95-mode quantitative outcomes, but it uses the available sources with distinct interpretations. The November 13 public dashboard snapshot contains 1,157 analyzed fit-test records and 769 distinct user-mask pairs across 57 users. Multiple tests of the same mask by the same person count as one user-mask pair; these are not independent participants. The source counts are 136 direct N95-mode, 150 N99-derived, 119 qualitative and 752 user-seal-check reports. Their proportions are 11.8, 13.0, 10.3 and 65.0 percent. These are record-level source categories after consolidation, not percentages of people who own each tool; repeat testing varies, and a participant may use multiple methods. The counts are not denominators for the separate 49-person or seven-person CV experiments. Other descriptive slides retain the explicitly dated October 29 snapshot of 1,145 records. Here fit means the project’s binary training outcome, not a claim that every source is an equivalent formal fit test. Direct N95-mode exercise factors are aggregated using a harmonic mean, with 100 or above labeled positive; the optional sealed exercise is excluded. For N99-mode records, broad-particle counts can reflect filter penetration as well as leakage. For intuition, a sealed N99-mode factor of 25 corresponds to 4 percent penetration, or 96 percent filtration. If the worn result is nearly 25 under comparable conditions, most measured penetration can be explained by the filter rather than seal leakage, yielding a higher estimated leakage-only N95-mode factor. This is a model-based inference, not a guaranteed fit pass; estimates become sensitive to reference uncertainty near the sealed limit. The project estimates leakage using filtration data, including Aaron Collins’s MaskNerd spreadsheet and contributed sealed-breathing observations. It pools sealed references per mask, adjusts an incompatible reference to the largest retained exercise factor plus one, excludes talking and sealed breathing, then multiplies the harmonic estimate by two. That factor is a sensitivity-oriented project adjustment, not an instrument-standard correction, and does not apply to direct N95-mode results. The appendix contains the equations and source attribution. The qualitative pathway requires all recorded nonblank results to pass and at least one explicit pass; this permissive completeness policy is not a claim of a fully completed OSHA protocol. An all-fail qualitative record without any explicit pass currently becomes unknown. Failed seal checks contribute proxy negatives; passing checks establish no positives. Negative overrides positive, then unknown. Unknown records are excluded by default. Incomplete exercises and mixed sources warrant label-quality review and formal-test-only sensitivity analysis. Mode names describe measurement methods, not a respirator’s certification class. The photos come from the presenter’s existing Breathesafe onboarding materials. They show a respirator inside a DIY bag hood, the hood opening, a nebulizer held at the opening during sensitivity screening, and the 8020A with N95 Companion. These are illustrations of equipment access, not a complete testing protocol or a demonstration that this DIY setup meets formal protocol requirements.

9. Keep the nose path separate from total perimeter. · 0:50
Previously, nose, upper cheek, mid-cheek and chin paths were summed into a single perimeter proxy. Two people with the same total could receive the same score despite different nose shapes. The perimeter-model logit is a times the squared signed perimeter difference, plus b times the difference, plus c and a strap-type offset. The signed difference is face perimeter minus mask perimeter, divided by ten to convert millimeters to centimeters. Face perimeter sums the nose, upper-cheek, mid-cheek and chin paths. Coefficients a, b and c combine learned fit-family and style terms; a is constrained negative. A logit is the model score before the sigmoid transformation; the displayed fit score is sigmoid of the new logit, not an independently calibrated probability. The new model retains the total-perimeter term and adds a separate squared nose mismatch. The face nose path is an input; the mask and style preferred nose distance is inferred from fit labels, not measured mask geometry. Across three initializations on the same five user-grouped folds, precision rose from 56.4% to 63.9%, recall rose from 58.0% to 59.4%, and log loss fell from 1.108 to 0.931. Precision asks: among tests called fitting, how many had positive labels? Recall asks: among positive labels, how many were found? NPV asks: among observations predicted not to fit, how many have negative labels? It rises from 85.7% to 86.6% at the fixed 50 percent cutoff. It is true negatives divided by true negatives plus false negatives; no predicted negatives makes it undefined. Log loss penalizes confident mistakes using the original continuous scores, not their binarized versions. Lower log loss means better overall probabilistic scoring but can reflect changes in discrimination or calibration; it does not establish calibration improvement alone. Higher precision at the fixed cutoff means a smaller false-positive fraction among predicted fits. It does not prove that scores fell for every nonfit, rose for every fit, or improved the absolute number of false positives or true positives; a more selective predictor can also improve precision. A recall interval including zero leaves the direction uncertain, rather than establishing equal recall. All classification metrics use the fixed 50 percent cutoff except log loss, which uses the scores directly. These are averages of pooled held-out metrics from each initialization, not training scores or independent replicated datasets. Brackets show pointwise 95 percent percentile intervals from 10,000 paired participant-cluster bootstrap samples: sample 49 people with replacement and retain all their tests, using identical draws for models and initializations. Retrain both classifiers from scratch in each original grouped-user fold with sampled participant multiplicities in the objective. Keep all copies of a person in the same fold. Compute each metric per initialization then average; do not average probabilities. These intervals include classifier training-population variation, conditional on fixed prepared inputs, category catalog, folds, hyperparameters and initialization bank. All five paired 95 percent difference intervals include zero. Log-loss strict wins are 86.41 percent and precision wins 84.71 percent, substantially less decisive than the earlier fixed-prediction bootstrap. See the appendix for paired difference intervals; separate model intervals cannot establish a difference. The mixed cohort contains measured and imputed ARKit paths. The regression used for imputation was fitted before CV, so full-pipeline generalization is not independently established. Measured-only training on 615 tests from 18 people did not reproduce the gain: average F1 fell from .620 to .578. The new log loss remains worse than the prevalence-only baseline of approximately .588; calibration needs separate reliability assessment. Labels mix evidence sources and repeated user-mask tests can remain. This is exploratory internal evidence. Shared nose sensitivity is now the training default in code; style-specific sensitivity remains experimental.

Coefficient context: The displayed coefficients combine a fit-family component and a mask-style component. The internal names alpha_mask_raw and beta_gamma_mask refer to fit-family categories in the current training pipeline. For the current constrained parameterization, the two raw alpha parameters are exponentiated and negated, then added, so the effective quadratic coefficient a is negative. The linear and intercept coefficients each add family and style values. Training learns all these underlying parameters jointly by minimizing binary cross-entropy on observed fit outcomes. The strap category contributes a separate additive offset. The with-nose model also subtracts a separately learned nose-mismatch penalty. The peak minus b over two a refers to the perimeter term with categories fixed; it is not a claim about the best physical mask geometry or the optimum of the full model when the nose term changes. The coefficients are learned associations, not physical constants. Legacy parameter files can bypass the newer negative-curvature transformation; the equation describes the current training parameterization.

12. Fixed-range inputs: which model wins after refitting? · 0:35
This is a new sensitivity comparison rather than a replacement for slide 10. The traditional models use prespecified physical ranges rather than training-sample z-scores. Each predictor is mapped by x minus fixed lower bound divided by the fixed range. Input values outside the bounds raise an error; they are not silently clipped. PCA is refitted within each training fold on scaled participant copies without whitening. That keeps input squares and interactions bounded, but it does not bound the learned logistic score or guarantee reliable extrapolation. 16. Next: validate measurements, then recommendation usefulness. · 0:20
This summary draws on the November 9 Blueprint Biosecurity detailed discussion draft, sections 5 and 6, plus the concept note. It describes proposed work; funding, recruitment and results are not established. The immediate priority is to freeze landmark and curve definitions, independent reference procedures, capture rules and the tape-derived scale process. A 20-adult geometry pilot tests repeatability and feasibility before expanding. The geometry cohort has 120 adults total: 60 development participants, including the 20 pilot participants, and 60 untouched test participants. All images, repeated sessions and derivatives of a participant remain in one partition. References are separately acquired physical measurements and independent scans where available; reconstruction source-photo agreement is not the sole validation reference. Use a second rater on a prespecified subset. Benchmark raw photogrammetry, zero-filter remeshing and smoothing, direct-photo-to-FLAME fitting, and ARKit where supported. Separately test style-specific features so capture improvements are not confounded with a new feature set. Report signed bias, mean and 95th-percentile absolute error in millimeters, repeatability, Bland-Altman agreement, reference uncertainty and complete capture/abstention denominators. Use participant-level resampling for repeated observations. Provisional geometry gates are at most 2 mm mean absolute error and 5 mm 95th-percentile absolute error for critical spans, and at most 5 mm mean absolute error for aggregate paths. These are engineering targets, to be finalized before opening the test set, not achieved accuracy claims. Evaluate about six Android devices, webcams and an iPhone reference, reserving at least one device model from development; target at least 90 percent capture completion within two attempts in supported conditions. Measure turnaround and processing cost rather than assuming the existing 61-photo workflow is acceptable for end users. If measurement validation succeeds, recruit 60 separate adults for a prospective quantitative fit-testing pilot. Freeze the recommender before outcomes, randomize testing order and blind the operator to recommendation source where practicable. Primary outcome is the paired difference in quantitative pass rate between the first camera recommendation and initial choice; secondary outcomes include at least one top-three pass and operational burden. This is a feasibility/effect-estimation pilot, not a powered equivalence or infection-prevention trial. The proposal begins with adults. Pediatric validation needs a separately scoped protocol; this summary does not claim that the planned adult cohort establishes child performance. Release a research beta only within validated conditions, with supported devices and explicit failure feedback. Publish methods, aggregate results, negative findings and proceed/narrow/stop decisions.

22. Which predictors enter the traditional models? · Backup
This is the input list used by both traditional comparison arms: face width, face length, bitragion-subnasale arc, nose protrusion, nasal root breadth, and nose bridge height, entered in millimeters. No age, sex, ethnicity or inferred ARKit measurements enter these classifiers. The direct model uses all six standardized dimensions. The PCA model uses the first three components of the same six standardized dimensions, without whitening. Three components were selected before outcome evaluation, not by choosing the best held-out score. This is a project-specific PCA, not the NIOSH PCA panel. The three components retained about 83.6 to 98.5 percent of training-person variance across folds. Both traditional models also receive mask fit-family, style, strap type and observed perimeter. Categories are one-hot encoded. The mask-perimeter scaler fits the distinct training mask/perimeter combinations; the perimeter and its square are included. The classifier includes shared linear and squared face or PC terms, those geometry terms interacting with each mask-category indicator, and geometry terms interacting with mask perimeter. Interactions allow mask rankings to vary with the face. These are not merely six dimensions entered into an otherwise face-independent mask ranking. L2 logistic regression uses C=1, deterministic lbfgs and at most 2,000 iterations. Each training person has equal total weight. 23. Both traditional models include squared terms. · Backup
Both traditional comparison models use linear and squared geometry terms. For the direct model, the six dimensions are standardized before squaring. For PCA, the same standardized dimensions are projected into three components, and those component scores are squared. This is not PCA applied to squared raw dimensions. The shared design includes one-hot fit-family, style and strap categories, standardized mask perimeter p and its square, each geometry basis term multiplied by p, and each geometry basis term multiplied by every category indicator. Thus the squared geometry terms also interact with perimeter and categories; this is not simply a full degree-two polynomial expansion. There are no explicit cross-products between different facial dimensions or different PCs. Squared PCs can nevertheless encode combinations of the original dimensions, because each PC is a linear combination. Both models are L2 logistic regression with C=1; coefficient signs are not constrained. This differs from the production ARKit architecture, which explicitly uses a concave quadratic in signed perimeter difference and a separate negative squared nose mismatch. Standardization and PCA are fitted using training people only.

24. From N99 counts to an estimated N95 fit factor. · Backup
Worked example: use final sealed reference 100, giving filtration efficiency 99 percent. An N99 exercise factor of 50 gives leakage 1 minus 0.98 divided by 0.99, or 0.010101. Its reciprocal is 99. For one retained exercise, the harmonic mean is 99, and the project multiplier gives 198. This is illustrative, not an observed participant result or direct N95 measurement. This is the equation used for the project’s N99-to-N95 estimate. F_i is the outside-to-inside particle ratio for an N99-mode exercise. E is the filtration fraction, not a percent; L_i is the inferred fraction of incoming flow that bypasses the filter. The simplified mixture gives one over F_i equals L_i plus one minus L_i times one minus E. Solving gives L_i equals one minus one minus one over F_i divided by E; its reciprocal is the exercise-level leakage estimate. Credit Aaron Collins, MaskNerd, for providing filtration-efficiency data at normal breathing rates through his Master Mask Testing Data Set spreadsheet. The repository selects imported entries identified as Aaron’s data; contributed Normal breathing SEALED measurements are a second source. For each imported E_j, compute an equivalent sealed ratio S_j equals one over one minus E_j. Contributed sealed ratios enter directly. Pool by mask using SQL UNION, which removes identical selected rows, then average the ratios, not efficiencies. 26. Which questionnaire answers become a failure? · Backup
This slide describes UserSealCheckService, the backend rule used to create recommender labels, rather than a formal fit-test protocol. The sizing question asks: What do you think about the sizing of this mask relative to your face? Its choices are Too big, Somewhere in-between too small and too big, and Too small. The second question asks, while performing a positive-pressure user seal check, how much air movement on your face along the seal of the mask did you feel? Its choices are A lot of air movement, Some air movement, and No air movement. Evaluation order matters. First, Too big or Too small returns fail immediately, even if the air answer is absent or contradicts it. Next, a blank or missing sizing or air answer returns incomplete. This means A lot of air movement without a sizing answer is incomplete, not fail. With both answers present, A lot of air movement returns fail, including when the nonblank sizing value is unrecognized. Middle sizing plus Some air movement or No air movement returns questionnaire pass. Other nonblank combinations return incomplete. Missing, empty or invalid JSON data is also incomplete. qlft_pass_value maps fail to false and both pass and incomplete to NULL. The label is therefore asymmetric: a failed self-report can contribute a proxy negative, but passing questions cannot contribute a positive fit label. Another source on the same test record may still supply a label; consolidation favors negative over positive over unknown. The interface also has negative-pressure questions, but those answers are not read by this backend label service. Some air movement being accepted here is a permissive project classification rule, not guidance that a leaking respirator passes a formal fit test. User seal checks are not substitutes for qualitative or quantitative fit tests. No questionnaire, service or training-label behavior is changed by this slide.

30. How the fixed-range, full-refit comparison works. · Backup
The fixed facial ranges were recorded before model outcomes were examined in this experiment. They are broad engineering assumptions, not externally validated percentiles. The mask upper limit was broadened from the initially proposed 650 mm to 800 mm after a predictor-only range check found two existing rows at 699 mm; no outcome was used to select limits. For PCA, a component with loadings v has a unit-cube minimum equal to the sum of negative loadings, and a width equal to the sum of their absolute values. Map the uncentered projection using those extrema; the learned PCA centering cancels when subtracting the component lower bound. This bounds scores for in-range inputs without fitting a score range to held-out faces.
