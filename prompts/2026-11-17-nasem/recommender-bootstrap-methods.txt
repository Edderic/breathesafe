Paired person-cluster bootstrap — November 14, 2026

Inputs: six saved out_of_fold_predictions.json files for variants none and
latent_nose_v1, seeds 42/43/44. Their comparison-report hashes match the
previously frozen recommender-evidence.json. Private prediction files stay in
tmp/retired-nasem-worktree-cache/recommender-style-nose-review; only aggregate
intervals, source hashes and method metadata are included in this deck.

49 people, 1,086 held-out tests. Match observations by person/test/mask/label
across models and initializations. Require unique test identities and one
held-out fold per person per prediction file. Reproduce all five point metrics
in recommender-evidence.json to absolute tolerance 1e-12 before bootstrapping.

Draw 49 people with replacement, 10,000 times, RNG seed 20261114 (NumPy
multinomial participant multiplicities, equivalent to uniform person draws).
Retain every selected person's tests, including multiplicity. Both models and
all initializations use the same participant draw. Preserve test weighting:
compute pooled confusion ratios and mean natural-log loss per initialization,
then average the three metric values. Do not ensemble probabilities or treat
initializations as independent samples. Classification threshold >=0.5;
log-loss correct-class probability clipped to [1e-15, 1-1e-15].

Pointwise percentile intervals: linear-interpolated 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles.
Paired difference = nose minus perimeter. Positive precision/recall/NPV/F1
and negative log-loss differences are favorable. Percentage metrics' differences
are shown in percentage points. Undefined ratio replicates are excluded per
metric; all 10,000 replicates were valid for every metric in this run.

Conditional on existing fitted models, folds and preprocessing. No bootstrap
retraining or fold selection. Shared training data across CV fits, global
pre-CV imputation, convenience sampling and mixed label sources remain
limitations. Masks are held as observed rather than a second bootstrap cluster.
No simultaneous-coverage or multiplicity correction. This does not establish
population generalization or fix bias in the original experiment.
